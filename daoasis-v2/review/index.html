Two limits on this build, stated up front. It ran in a cloud session that could reach the website repository but not your computer or Google Drive. So (1) the App V2 screens are a working HTML prototype in the app's own visual language, built from the app screens already shipped on the website. They are not edits to the App Design-Claude source files, which still need porting once approved. (2) The existing competitor research in the Master folder was not available, so the benchmark in section 2 is a light public check, not that research.

01What changed

  • The Journey became a system, not a single route. One data file now describes every Journey in the same shape: Everest Base Camp (flagship), the Thailand Journey (launch, kept), Land's End to John o' Groats (new example), an optional 30-day Foundation Journey (concept), and three structured placeholders for the Camino de Santiago, the Nakasendo and Kilimanjaro.
  • Progress goes beyond steps. Three layers: Route (movement), Readiness (sleep, breathing, hydration, recovery) and Discovery (learning, reflection). Marked as a concept: no scoring formula is proposed.
  • Rest became part of the Journey. Everest has two acclimatisation stages where the route waits and readiness builds.
  • App V2: Home and Journey updated, plus five new screens: Today on the Journey, Stage detail, The route, Milestones, Choose a Journey.
  • Website V2: a new Journeys page; a new Journeys section on the home page; targeted wording changes on the home and App pages.
  • New imagery: route maps, an altitude profile and ridgeline plates, all generated from the Journey data, plus exported App V2 screens.

02Why it changed

Until now the Journey was one route, Bangkok to Phuket, and the copy said it plainly: "Your steps are the engine." That puts DAOasis in the same category as every virtual walking app. Those apps turn steps into a line on a map and reward the line with medals, stamps, postcards and landmark facts. A light public check of the category (Walking Planet, Walk the World, Virtual Walking and similar) shows the same pattern: steps are the only input, and arriving is the only reward.

V2 keeps the part people already understand, a real route and a marker that moves, and adds what only DAOasis does:

  • Everyday behaviour beyond steps moves the Journey: sleep, breath, water, food, learning and reflection.
  • Rest is progress. No virtual walking app rewards slowing down. Everest's acclimatisation stages make that the headline, and directly discourage overactivity.
  • Every stage teaches something and asks a reflective question, so the place is more than a postcard.
  • One architecture for every Journey, so the library can grow without app redesign.
  • The community walks the same routes, with a shared marker, and the Thailand Journey still arrives where the Sanctuary is planned.

03What stayed exactly the same

Brand, logo, colour palette, typography, navigation, every hero section, every animation, the Sanctuary and Web3 positioning, tokenomics, the business model and all investor and planning documents.

In the website copy, eleven of the thirteen existing pages are unchanged: Sanctuary, Web3, Investors, About and the seven trust pages. Each V2 copy differs from production by exactly three things, all review scaffolding: a "do not index" tag, the removal of the canonical link (so the copy never claims to be the real site), and a small "Journey V2 · review copy" tag in the corner. Only index.html and app.html carry content changes, and a new journeys.html was added.

Production is untouched: nothing outside the daoasis-v2/ folder was modified.

04New Journey architecture

Every Journey is one entry in daoasis-v2/journeys/journeys.json. The App screens, the website maps and the altitude profile are all drawn from that file by one shared generator (tools/journey-art.js). Nothing is hard-coded to Everest. To prove it, the App V2 page re-renders all seven screens for any Journey at the press of a button.

FieldWhat it holds
Identityname, short name, region, role (flagship / launch / example / concept), description, digital-notice line
Distancetotal kilometres, with a note where the figure needs a decision
Stagesname, km, altitude (if any), real coordinates, type (start / stop / rest / destination), title, short story, lesson, reflection prompt, milestone
Landmarkssmaller places along the way, with km, altitude and a one-line note
Peaksoptional, for mountain Journeys; drawn on the map
Milestonesnamed achievements at set distances
Behaviour emphasiswhich habits matter most on this route (Everest: sleep, breathing, hydration, recovery, learning)
Rewardsrecognition only. DRC amounts are not set and nothing implies a price or yield
Imagerywhich plate to use, and the photography status
Demoan illustrative member state, used only by the prototype

The progress model (concept, requires approval)

LayerThe questionFed by
RouteWhere am I?Movement
ReadinessAm I ready for what comes next?Sleep · Breathing · Hydration · Recovery
DiscoveryWhat am I taking from this place?Learning · Reflection

Four principles sit with it: rest stages advance readiness rather than distance; a comfortable daily distance applies, and going past it carries you no further; consistency is a rhythm, never a streak that resets; and every Journey is digital, inspired by a real route.

Not approved, not a formula. Readiness percentages and distances on the screens are illustrative demo states. The existing app already has a "distance boost" for good sleep. V2 extends that idea; it does not replace it, and no maths has been invented.

05Everest implementation

Lukla to Everest Base Camp, 65 km, seven stages, two rest stages. The route on the map is drawn from each place's real coordinates, with Everest, Lhotse, Nuptse, Pumori and Ama Dablam placed where they are.

Seven landmarks fill the gaps (Phakding, Monjo, Jorsale, Phunki Tenga, Deboche, Pangboche, Thukla Pass), so the altitude profile shows the real dips, such as the descent to Phunki Tenga before the climb to Tengboche. Each stage has a short story, a lesson (for example "How the body adapts to altitude — and why slower is faster") and a reflection prompt.

How it shows acclimatisation: at Namche and Dingboche the route stops. The Home screen says "Rest is part of the climb." Readiness builds from sleep, breath, water and recovery, and the next stage opens at 100%, with the line "Pushing harder will not open it sooner."

To check before production: distances and altitudes are rounded from published trekking guides (sources vary by a few km). Stage stories should be read by someone who knows the Khumbu, especially the memorials near Lobuche and Tengboche Monastery, and every Everest page states it is a digital Journey, not travel or trekking advice.

06Thailand implementation

Retained exactly as published: Bangkok 0 · Ayutthaya 76 · Hua Hin 199 · Surat Thani 544 · Khao Lak 782 · Phuket 900 km. It is now called the Thailand Journey and labelled the launch Journey. It is the first Journey in the library, built on the same architecture as Everest, and it still arrives where the Sanctuary is planned. The App V2 page renders all seven screens for Thailand with no special case.

Ranong. The V2 brief listed Ranong between Hua Hin and Surat Thani. It is not on the published route (website, app screens and investor materials all use the six above), and it sits on the opposite coast to Surat Thani, so adding it would make the line zig-zag across the peninsula. It has not been added. See open decisions.

07Land's End to John o' Groats

Added as the example of a Journey in another country, running south to north as the brief asked: Land's End · Barnstaple · Knighton · Hebden Bridge · Jedburgh · Fort William · John o' Groats. These waypoints follow the waymarked End to End Trail, through the South West, the Welsh borders, the Pennines, the Scottish Borders and the Highlands.

The distance is 1,407 km, the figure already published on the App page. The waymarked walking trail is about 1,956 km, so the stage kilometres are indicative (spaced in proportion along the trail) and marked as such. This Journey is about consistency rather than altitude, so it has no rest stages.

08Future Journey architecture

The Camino de Santiago (800 km), the Nakasendo (534 km) and Kilimanjaro (65 km) are in the data file as structured placeholders. Each has a name, region and distance, and is ready for stages. They appear as "In development" on the website and "Coming" in the app. Adding Journey 20 means filling in one entry: no screen or page changes.

Optional: the DAOasis Foundation Journey (concept)

A 30-day, 90 km teaching Journey with no geography: Week 1 Begin, Week 2 Rest, Week 3 Breathe, Week 4 Arrive. It would teach how Journeys work (movement carries you, rest opens the way, learning tells the story) before someone picks a real route. It is in the app prototype only, marked "Concept · optional", and kept off the website. Recommendation: offer it, never require it.

09Website changes

New page · journeys.html

Built on the About page's existing design (same nav, footer, dividers, type and reveal style; no new animation system). In order: the hero "Imagine walking the best parts of the world, from anywhere.", then What a Journey is (the three layers), then How a Journey unfolds (Choose · Live · Progress · Discover · Connect · Arrive · Continue), then The flagship: Everest Base Camp (route map, altitude profile, seven stages), then Rest is part of the Journey, then Everest is one Journey. There will be many., then Your Journey lives in the Companion App, then the closing call to action.

It is not added to the main navigation; it is reached from the new home section and the App page. See open decisions.

Home page · index.html

  • New "Journeys" section after the Ecosystem plates, on the same ivory ground: the core statement, one line of explanation, then three equal cards with Everest first as the flagship, Thailand second and Land's End third. It closes with "Everest is one Journey…" and a link to the Journeys page. Everest does not dominate the page.
  • Ecosystem line: "Every habit moves the same Journey — Bangkok to Phuket is the launch Journey…" becomes "Every habit moves your Journey — across Thailand, up to Everest Base Camp and beyond…"
  • "Track what matters" (two places): "One marker, nine hundred kilometres" becomes "One marker on a real route — nine hundred kilometres across Thailand, sixty-five to Everest Base Camp."
  • The member-lifecycle section's kicker, "The journey", becomes "Your path through DAOasis" (desktop and mobile). It describes joining, earning and governance, not a route, and two meanings of "Journey" on one page would confuse people.

App page · app.html

  • Hero bridge line: "The Bangkok → Phuket Journey awaits" becomes "From Thailand to Everest Base Camp — your Journey awaits".
  • "The simple version": lede becomes "The everyday part of DAOasis — and where your Journey lives." Step 02 "Stay consistent" becomes "Move your Journey — movement, rest and learning carry you through extraordinary routes…"
  • "How it works" tag, scroll-map kicker and map header now say The Thailand Journey.
  • "Your steps are the engine — and every other habit you build makes you faster" becomes "Movement carries you — and every other habit you build carries you further."
  • The six-card section is now "The Journey library", with new body copy and a link to the Journeys page. The Everest card is now "Flagship Journey", 65 km, with acclimatisation copy (it said 130 km and "amplified rewards"). The UK card now runs Land's End → John o' Groats (it ran the other way).
  • The scroll-driven Thailand map, the Living Ecosystem and every animation are untouched.

11New imagery

  • Route maps: drawn from each stage's real coordinates on a contour field, with peaks for mountain routes. Generated from the data, so they update when the data does.
  • Altitude profile for Everest, from stage and landmark altitudes.
  • Ridgeline plates: generated dusk artwork in the DAOasis palette (images/v2/ridge-*.jpg, 26–46 KB).
  • App V2 screen exports (images/v2/app-v2-*.webp, 45–87 KB), used on the website.
  • Reused existing photography: journey-image.jpg for Thailand.
The ridgeline plates are a stand-in, not the finished look. They are honest artwork, but they are not photography, and next to the Thailand photograph they read as flatter. DAOasis has no Himalayan or British photography, and none could be sourced from this session. Production needs licensed or commissioned photorealistic images for the Everest hero, each Everest stage and Land's End to John o' Groats, rendered to match the existing Thailand set.

12Open decisions

  1. The progress model. Route / Readiness / Discovery, rest stages that pause distance, and a comfortable daily cap: are these the direction? Then the scoring needs to be designed properly.
  2. Everest distance: 65 km to Base Camp, with the descent as an optional continuation (making the previously published 130 km), or the full 130 km return as one Journey?
  3. Ranong on the Thailand Journey: leave it off (recommended), or re-route the published Journey? Re-routing changes the app, website and investor route together.
  4. Land's End to John o' Groats distance: keep the published 1,407 km, or use the walking trail's ~1,956 km? Stage km must be set from the final route either way.
  5. Foundation Journey: include as an optional first Journey, or drop?
  6. Journeys page in the navigation: add "Journeys" to the nav and footer, or keep it reached from Home and App only?
  7. The word "journey" in two senses: agree "Your path through DAOasis" for the member lifecycle section.
  8. Photography: commission or license (see section 11).
  9. Other existing Journey cards (Camino, Nakasendo, Kilimanjaro) still mention "bonuses", "multipliers" and "special rewards". They were left unchanged as out of scope, but should be brought in line with "recognition, not rewards with a price".
  10. Porting the App screens into the App Design-Claude source once approved.
